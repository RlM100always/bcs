## 2. What the trend actually says

### Priority A — master these first
1. **Forest and biodiversity conservation:** 35th, 41st and related environmental questions.
2. **Minerals and energy resources:** 37th, 38th and 41st (gas).
3. **Blue Economy and marine resources:** 35th, 38th, 40th, 41st (cross-paper), 44th and 46th.
4. **Agriculture, land and food security:** 40th, 44th, 45th and 46th.
5. **Sustainable development and environmental governance:** 41st, 43rd, 46th and 47th.
6. **Water-resource management:** 45th (Teesta) and 47th (cross-paper source protection), with BDP 2100 and IWRM as frameworks.

### Priority B — prepare as integrated applications
- ECA, protected areas and ecosystem services.
- Soil degradation, agricultural land conversion and sustainable farming.
- Fish production, breeding grounds, hilsa management and livelihoods.
- Climate resilience, natural disasters, drought, salinity and coastal adaptation.
- Renewable energy, energy efficiency, circular economy and responsible extraction.
- Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA), public consultation and transparent monitoring.

## 3. Answer patterns by question verb

| Question verb | What the examiner expects |
|---|---|
| **Describe / identify** | Define the resource, classify it, give a Bangladesh-specific example and state its use. |
| **Explain / discuss** | Add causes, economic/social/ecological importance, constraints and consequences. |
| **Analyse** | Show causal links and trade-offs: extraction → jobs/revenue → ecological/social costs → governance response. |
| **Evaluate** | Weigh benefits against limits; distinguish policy intention from verified outcomes. |
| **Suggest measures** | Give practical steps, responsible institutions, funding/technology, local participation and measurable indicators. |

## 4. A repeatable 20-mark answer framework

1. **Introduction (2–3 sentences):** define the resource and state why it matters to Bangladesh.
2. **Resource base and use:** location/type, major users, economic or livelihood role.
3. **Problem diagnosis:** ecological degradation, access/equity, technology, enforcement, market and climate pressures.
4. **Critical evaluation:** trade-offs, affected communities, short- versus long-term outcomes.
5. **Recommendations:** 5–7 feasible actions, with relevant institutions and monitoring.
6. **Conclusion:** link sustainable use with ecological integrity, resilience and intergenerational equity.
