> **Scope and evidence:** This audit checks the Bangladesh Affairs (Subject Code 005) themes that are relevant to elections, the Election Commission, electoral law, observation, representation and democratic political culture. It is not a claim that every question from every paper has been authenticated against a BPSC-issued original scan. The main public archive explicitly states that its questions are compiled from secondary sources and may contain omissions or transcription errors. Where an ordinary Bangladesh Affairs paper has no relevant question identifiable in the consulted archive, that is stated as “not identified in the accessible archive”, not “never asked”.

## 2. What repeats across the period?

### Priority A — prepare in depth
1. **Election Commission: constitutional composition, independence and functions** — directly reported in the 35th, 41st and 43rd BCS Bangladesh Affairs papers.
2. **Election observers and election missions** — directly reported in the 36th, 37th and 41st BCS papers.
3. **RPO and campaign finance** — directly reported as an RPO short note in the 36th and as election-expense themes in the 35th and 37th.
4. **Elections and democracy** — directly reported in the 43rd; broader political-culture analysis appears in the 47th.
5. **Women’s representation and reserved seats** — related question in the 38th.
6. **1970 general election** — historical electoral mandate question in the 37th and 40th.

### Priority B — prepare as analytical extensions
- Articles 118–126 of the Constitution.
- Electoral rolls, adult franchise and equal participation.
- Constituency delimitation and published legal boundaries.
- Nomination, affidavits and candidate disclosure under current law.
- Campaign finance: ceiling, reporting, audit and equal enforcement.
- Secret ballot, transparent counting and result publication.
- Election petitions, complaint handling and remedies.
- Deepfakes, AI-generated content, social media and verified public information.
- Accessibility for women, older persons and persons with disabilities.
- The relationship between electoral credibility and democratic political culture.

## 3. Why the 2026 law matters

The **Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act No. 03 of 2026, dated 10 April 2026)** is available in the official Laws of Bangladesh database. It states that it is deemed effective from 3 November 2025. Study its current provisions alongside the consolidated RPO; do not rely on a superseded 2025 ordinance or an older coaching-book summary.

Topics to revise from the official text include the defined “No Vote” procedure for a sole candidate, specified postal-ballot provisions, nomination/disclosure requirements, campaign-expenditure rules and provisions addressing certain election-related misleading or synthetic content. The exact legal conditions matter; do not overgeneralise a rule beyond its wording.

- [Official 2026 Amendment Act](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1632.html)
- [Official consolidated RPO](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-424.html)
- [EC’s national parliamentary election-law register](https://ecs.gov.bd/en/page/national-parliament-election-law)

## 4. Current expenditure rule versus historical comparison

Article 44B(3) of the current consolidated RPO states that a contesting candidate’s election expenses—including expenditure incurred for the candidate by the nominating party—must not exceed **Tk 10 per voter or Tk 25 lakh, whichever is higher**. This is the current rule reflected in the official consolidated law.

The 35th BCS question asks for a historical sequence beginning in 2001. To produce a defensible historical graph, use each official amendment/gazette in force at the relevant election and record the effective date and the exact legal formula. Do not simply copy figures from undated guides; a fixed ceiling and a per-voter formula are not directly comparable. The current limit must not be retroactively applied to older elections.

## 5. How to answer by command word

| Command word | What the examiner expects |
|---|---|
| **Define / What is** | A precise definition, legal basis and one line on significance. |
| **Describe / Explain** | Main provisions or stages in logical order, with relevant constitutional articles. |
| **Discuss / Analyse** | Causes, institutional roles, strengths, limitations, consequences and competing considerations. |
| **Evaluate / Critically assess** | A balanced judgement supported by criteria and evidence—not only a list of problems. |
| **Recommend / Suggest** | Feasible reforms, responsible institutions, implementation steps and measurable indicators. |

## 6. A reusable 20-mark answer structure

1. **Introduction:** define the issue and establish its relevance to Bangladesh.
2. **Constitutional/legal basis:** cite the relevant article or current statute.
3. **Institution and process:** explain who does what and at which stage.
4. **Critical analysis:** identify strengths, weaknesses, risks and affected groups.
5. **Evidence:** use official laws, election records or a dated statistic where relevant.
6. **Recommendations:** give 5–7 practical steps and, where possible, indicators.
7. **Conclusion:** link the recommendations to legality, inclusion, transparency and public trust.

## 7. Source-quality notes

- The public [BCS EXAM archive](https://bcsexam.org/written-questions) says its collection is secondary and may contain omissions or transcription errors; it is not a substitute for authenticated original BPSC scans.
- The 37th BCS question copy cited here is hosted on Scribd and its OCR contains obvious errors. It is useful for identifying themes but should not be represented as an official scan.
- **39th and 42nd BCS were special examinations**, so they are not counted as ordinary Bangladesh Affairs written papers.
- “No direct question identified” for 44th–46th means none was confirmed in the consulted archive section; it does not prove that no related question exists in every source or variant.
- The official BPSC syllabus explicitly includes “Elections in Bangladesh”, the role of the EC, electoral law, campaigns, RPO and election-observation teams. These remain syllabus topics even when a particular year’s question bank does not show a direct question.
