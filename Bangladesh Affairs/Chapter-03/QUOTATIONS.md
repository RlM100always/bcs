## A. Short historical quotations — direct or widely documented

### 1. The 7 March Speech — Sheikh Mujibur Rahman (1971)
> “The struggle this time is the struggle for our emancipation; the struggle this time is the struggle for independence.”

**বাংলা অর্থ:** এবারের সংগ্রাম আমাদের মুক্তির সংগ্রাম; এবারের সংগ্রাম স্বাধীনতার সংগ্রাম।\
**কোথায় ব্যবহার করবেন:** ভাষণ, অসহযোগ আন্দোলন, মুক্তিযুদ্ধের পটভূমি, রাজনৈতিক নেতৃত্ব ও গণসংগঠন।\
**Source:** UNESCO, Memory of the World — *The Historic 7th March Speech of Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman*; speech recording/transcript and official historical records.\
https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world

### 2. Proclamation of Independence (10 April 1971)
> “We, the elected representatives of the people of Bangladesh, whose will is supreme duly constituted ourselves into a Constituent Assembly…”

**বাংলা অর্থ:** বাংলাদেশের জনগণের নির্বাচিত প্রতিনিধিরা জনগণের ইচ্ছাকে সর্বোচ্চ ধরে নিজেদের গণপরিষদ হিসেবে গঠন করেন—ঘোষণাপত্রের বক্তব্যের অংশ।\
**কোথায় ব্যবহার করবেন:** মুজিবনগর সরকার, স্বাধীনতার আইনগত ভিত্তি, নির্বাচনী ম্যান্ডেট ও রাষ্ট্রগঠন।\
**Source:** *Proclamation of Independence, 10 April 1971*; Banglapedia, “Constitutional Development”; Government of Bangladesh Liberation War records.\
https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Constitutional_Development\
**নোট:** পরীক্ষায় হুবহু উদ্ধৃতি দেওয়ার আগে মূল ঘোষণাপত্রের সরকারি/বিশ্বস্ত প্রতিলিপির সঙ্গে বানান ও শব্দচয়ন মিলিয়ে নিন।

### 3. “Memory of the World” — UNESCO designation
এটি কোনো বক্তার উদ্ধৃতি নয়; UNESCO-এর একটি documentary-heritage programme-এর নাম। ৭ মার্চের ভাষণকে আন্তর্জাতিক documentary heritage হিসেবে ব্যাখ্যা করতে এই designation উল্লেখ করুন, কিন্তু quotation হিসেবে উপস্থাপন করবেন না।\
**Source:** UNESCO Memory of the World Register.\
https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world

## B. Source-backed English exam lines
নিচের বাক্যগুলো প্রবন্ধে ব্যবহারযোগ্য evidence-based summaries। এগুলো **paraphrase**, সরাসরি ঐতিহাসিক quote নয়।

### Ancient and medieval Bengal
1. **“The history of Bengal is best understood through changing regional polities, riverine geography, agrarian expansion and cultural interaction—not through fixed modern boundaries projected backwards.”**\
   **Use for:** ancient janapadas, geography and state formation.\
   **Source:** Banglapedia, historical entries on ancient Bengal and janapadas. https://en.banglapedia.org/

2. **“Archaeological sites such as Mahasthangarh, Paharpur and Mainamati provide material evidence of settlement, religious patronage, learning and regional connections.”**\
   **Use for:** archaeology, Pala period and cultural heritage.\
   **Source:** UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Paharpur; Banglapedia. https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/322/

3. **“Bengal’s cultural history reflects both continuity and change, shaped by multiple religious traditions, languages, political institutions and patterns of exchange.”**\
   **Use for:** medieval culture, syncretism and identity.\
   **Source:** Banglapedia, cultural-history entries. https://en.banglapedia.org/

### Colonial rule and partition
4. **“Colonial rule transformed land revenue, administration, markets and political institutions while primarily serving the interests of imperial governance.”**\
   **Use for:** Plassey, Diwani, Permanent Settlement and colonial economy.\
   **Source:** Banglapedia, entries on Plassey, Permanent Settlement and colonial Bengal. https://en.banglapedia.org/

5. **“The Partition of Bengal in 1905 generated different political responses across regions and communities, making its legacy more complex than a single narrative of opposition or support.”**\
   **Use for:** Bengal Partition, Swadeshi movement and regional politics.\
   **Source:** Banglapedia, “Partition of Bengal, 1905.” https://en.banglapedia.org/

6. **“The Partition of 1947 placed East Bengal within Pakistan but left unresolved questions of linguistic recognition, political representation and economic equity.”**\
   **Use for:** 1947, East-West disparities and Language Movement.\
   **Source:** Banglapedia, entries on Partition and Language Movement. https://en.banglapedia.org/

### Language Movement and autonomy
7. **“The Language Movement turned the question of state language into a broader demand for cultural recognition, democratic participation and political equality.”**\
   **Use for:** 1952, nationalism and civic rights.\
   **Source:** Banglapedia, “Language Movement”; BPSC BCS Written Syllabus. https://en.banglapedia.org/

8. **“The United Front victory of 1954 expressed public discontent with central rule and strengthened the demand for provincial autonomy.”**\
   **Use for:** 1954 election, regional politics and representation.\
   **Source:** Banglapedia, entries on the 1954 election and United Front. https://en.banglapedia.org/

9. **“The Six-Point Programme connected constitutional autonomy with fiscal authority, economic safeguards and political representation.”**\
   **Use for:** Six-Point Movement and East-West disparities.\
   **Source:** Banglapedia, “Six-Point Programme.” https://en.banglapedia.org/

10. **“The 1970 election produced a clear democratic mandate, but the failure to transfer power deepened the constitutional and political crisis.”**\
    **Use for:** election, non-cooperation and background to the Liberation War.\
    **Source:** Banglapedia, entries on the 1970 election and Liberation War. https://en.banglapedia.org/

### Liberation War and Mujibnagar Government
11. **“The 7 March Speech linked the electoral mandate to mass mobilisation, non-cooperation and preparation for resistance.”**\
    **Use for:** speech, March 1971 and political leadership.\
    **Source:** UNESCO Memory of the World; Banglapedia. https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world

12. **“The Proclamation of Independence and the Mujibnagar Government gave the Liberation War a formal political and administrative framework.”**\
    **Use for:** government formation, legal basis and statehood.\
    **Source:** Banglapedia, “Mujibnagar Government” and “Constitutional Development.” https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?lang=en&title=Mujibnagar_Government

13. **“The Liberation War was sustained by the agency of the people of Bangladesh, organised resistance, wartime leadership and international developments.”**\
    **Use for:** balanced evaluation of the war and the role of external powers.\
    **Source:** Banglapedia; Ministry of Liberation War Affairs; FRUS archival records.\
    https://molwa.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/694032b335ce18e1c0560d16\
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v11

14. **“International responses to the 1971 crisis were shaped by humanitarian concerns as well as strategic interests and Cold War alignments.”**\
    **Use for:** India, United States, Soviet Union, China and the United Nations.\
    **Source:** Office of the Historian, U.S. Department of State, *FRUS 1969–1976, Volume XI: South Asia Crisis, 1971*.\
    https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v11

### Post-independence history and culture
15. **“State-building after 1971 required not only political sovereignty but also institutional reconstruction, rehabilitation, economic recovery and constitutional settlement.”**\
    **Use for:** post-independence challenges and constitution-making.\
    **Source:** Banglapedia, “Constitutional Development”; BPSC syllabus. https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Constitutional_Development

16. **“A responsible national history recognises the centrality of the Liberation War while examining evidence, regional experiences and the diversity of people’s contributions.”**\
    **Use for:** history writing, memory, heritage and inclusive identity.\
    **Source:** Ministry of Liberation War Affairs; Bangladesh National Museum; archival and oral-history methodology.\
    https://molwa.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/694032b335ce18e1c0560d16

## C. How to use a quotation in a BCS Written answer

**Good pattern:**
1. Make your argument in your own words.
2. Add one short quote or source-backed line only if it directly supports the point.
3. Name the source and the document/year.
4. Explain its relevance in the next sentence.

**Example:**
The 1970 election produced a clear democratic mandate, but the failure to transfer power deepened the political crisis. This helps explain why the March 1971 Non-Cooperation Movement became a mass political response rather than an isolated protest. *(Source: Banglapedia, entries on the 1970 election and Liberation War.)*

**Avoid:**
- Inventing quotes or assigning paraphrases to historical leaders.
- Using quotations as a substitute for analysis.
- Quoting long passages from copyrighted modern works.
- Writing “UNESCO said…” when the sentence is your own summary.


## D. Additional authoritative quotations for flexible exam use

> **Citation caution:** English translations of Bangla historical documents can vary between editions. Keep the source/document name beside the quotation and do not attribute the original English wording to a speaker unless the cited transcript supports it.

### 4. Bangladesh Constitution — Preamble
> “We, the people of Bangladesh, having proclaimed our Independence on the 26th day of March, 1971…”

**Source:** *The Constitution of the People’s Republic of Bangladesh*, Preamble, official English text.\
**Use:** state formation, popular sovereignty, constitutionalism and post-independence reconstruction.\
**Official source:** https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-367.html

### 5. Bangladesh Constitution — language and national identity
> “The state language of the Republic is Bangla.”

**Source:** *The Constitution of the People’s Republic of Bangladesh*, Article 3, official English text.\
**Use:** Language Movement, linguistic identity and constitutional recognition.\
**Official source:** https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-367.html

### 6. UNESCO — cultural diversity
> “Cultural diversity is as necessary for humankind as biodiversity is for nature.”

**Source:** UNESCO, *Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity* (2001), Article 1.\
**Use:** language, folk culture, indigenous communities, plural identity and cultural heritage.\
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/unesco-universal-declaration-cultural-diversity

### 7. UNESCO — safeguarding heritage
> “The deterioration or disappearance of any item of the cultural or natural heritage constitutes a harmful impoverishment of the heritage of all the nations of the world.”

**Source:** UNESCO, *Convention Concerning the Protection of the World Cultural and Natural Heritage* (1972), Preamble.\
**Use:** Paharpur, Bagerhat, archaeological conservation and heritage protection.\
**Source:** https://whc.unesco.org/en/conventiontext/

### 8. Universal Declaration of Human Rights — freedom of expression
> “Everyone has the right to freedom of opinion and expression…”

**Source:** United Nations, *Universal Declaration of Human Rights* (1948), Article 19.\
**Use:** Language Movement, press freedom, cultural expression and democratic rights.\
**Source:** https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights

### 9. Universal Declaration of Human Rights — cultural participation
> “Everyone has the right freely to participate in the cultural life of the community…”

**Source:** United Nations, *Universal Declaration of Human Rights* (1948), Article 27(1).\
**Use:** folk culture, cultural rights, public access to heritage and inclusive national identity.\
**Source:** https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights

### 10. UNESCO — safeguarding living heritage
> “The ‘intangible cultural heritage’ means the practices, representations, expressions, knowledge, skills…”

**Source:** UNESCO, *Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage* (2003), Article 2(1).\
**Use:** Baul songs, Mangal Shobhajatra, oral traditions, crafts and community-based heritage.\
**Source:** https://ich.unesco.org/en/convention

### 11. United Nations Charter — peace and international cooperation
> “to save succeeding generations from the scourge of war…”

**Source:** *Charter of the United Nations* (1945), Preamble.\
**Use:** international context of the Liberation War, refugees, diplomacy and the role of international institutions.\
**Source:** https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text

### 12. The Historic 7 March Speech — concise anchor
> “The struggle this time is the struggle for our emancipation; the struggle this time is the struggle for independence.”

**Source:** Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, 7 March 1971 speech; UNESCO Memory of the World and preserved speech records.\
**Use:** mass mobilisation, non-cooperation, political leadership and the path to independence.\
**Source:** https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world

### 13. Proclamation of Independence — popular mandate
> “We, the elected representatives of the people of Bangladesh…”

**Source:** *Proclamation of Independence*, 10 April 1971. Use the complete text when discussing the legal and political foundation of the Mujibnagar Government.\
**Use:** election mandate, state formation and constitutional legitimacy.\
**Reference:** Banglapedia, “Constitutional Development.” https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Constitutional_Development

### 14. UNESCO — culture as a shared responsibility
> “The cultural heritage of each is the cultural heritage of all.”

**Source note:** This wording is a concise **exam-ready paraphrase**, not a verified verbatim UNESCO quotation. Do not put it in quotation marks in an exam; write it as your own summary and cite the 1972 World Heritage Convention instead.\
**Use:** shared responsibility for preservation and international heritage cooperation.

## E. Best quotation choices by question

| Question theme | Best quotation / source | How to connect it |
|---|---|---|
| Language Movement and national identity | Constitution, Article 3; UDHR Article 19 | Explain language as identity and a question of expression and recognition |
| 7 March Speech and Liberation War | 7 March Speech; Proclamation of Independence | Connect mass mobilisation to political mandate and state formation |
| Mujibnagar Government | Proclamation of Independence, 10 April 1971 | Explain the legal-political framework of the war |
| Paharpur, Bagerhat and archaeology | UNESCO World Heritage Convention (1972) | Argue that conservation protects shared human heritage |
| Baul, Mangal Shobhajatra and folk culture | UNESCO Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity (2001); ICH Convention (2003) | Connect living traditions with diversity and community participation |
| Post-independence state-building | Bangladesh Constitution, Preamble | Link independence, popular sovereignty and constitutional order |
| International dimension of 1971 | UN Charter, Preamble | Discuss peace, international cooperation and humanitarian concerns |

**Exam rule:** One well-connected quotation is usually enough for a 10- or 20-mark answer. The next sentence must explain its relevance to the question; do not use a quote as a substitute for historical evidence.
