## Method and evidence level

The public archive at [BCS EXAM — Written Questions](https://bcsexam.org/written-questions) lists question sets from 35th through 50th BCS, but explicitly warns that the collection is a third-party archive and is not transcribed directly from BPSC's original question-paper scans in every case. Therefore, this audit distinguishes **archive-reported question wording** from **syllabus-led practice themes**. It does not claim to be an official BPSC-certified frequency analysis.

## Directly relevant archive-reported examples

### 43rd BCS Written — Bangladesh Affairs, Question 21
The public archive records these questions:
- “জনশুমারি কী?” (5 marks)
- “ক্ষুদ্র নৃ-গোষ্ঠী বলতে কী বোঝায়?” (5 marks)
- “বাংলাদেশের জাতিগত ও সাংস্কৃতিক বৈচিত্র্য বর্ণনা করুন।” (10 marks)

**Why it matters:** This is a direct, explicit match to Chapter 02's syllabus scope. Prepare a definition of census, distinguish ethnic identity from citizenship, and write an evidence-based account of Bangladesh's ethnic and cultural diversity.  
**Archive source:** https://bcsexam.org/written-questions/43rd-bcs-written  
**Evidence label:** Archive-reported; verify against the original BPSC scan if available.

### 41st BCS Written — Bangladesh Affairs, Question 25
The public archive records:
- Explain the interrelationship of education, society and the economy in Bangladesh's overall development (10 marks).
- Explain how Bangladesh can convert its large population into human resources for economic development, with examples (10 marks).

**Why it matters:** This directly supports demographic dividend, human-capital formation, education, skills and employment as core answer themes.  
**Archive source:** https://bcsexam.org/written-questions/41st-bcs-written  
**Evidence label:** Archive-reported; verify against the original BPSC scan if available.

### 47th BCS Written — Bangladesh Affairs, Question 27
The public archive records a comparative question on socio-economic and cultural changes between Bangladesh in the 1970s and in 2025, with particular attention to women's work, urbanisation, language and ICT use (20 marks).

**Why it matters:** Connect demographic and cultural change to women's labour-force participation, urbanisation, technology, family and language practices. Treat it as a comparative essay, not a list of disconnected facts.  
**Archive source:** https://bcsexam.org/written-questions/47th-bcs-written  
**Evidence label:** Archive-reported; verify against the original BPSC scan if available.

### 35th BCS Written — cross-paper nation-building theme
The public archive records a question in International Affairs asking candidates to evaluate Bangladesh's successes and failures in building a political nation and recommend ways to strengthen the spirit of the Liberation War (15 marks).

**Why it matters to Chapter 02:** This is a cross-paper connection to national identity, citizenship and social cohesion, not a direct demographic-statistics question. Use it as a related essay theme only.  
**Archive source:** https://bcsexam.org/written-questions/35th-bcs-written  
**Evidence label:** Archive-reported; verify against the original BPSC scan if available.

### 41st BCS Written — cross-paper Rohingya and gender themes
The archive also includes Rohingya displacement in a translation task and women's empowerment in Bangladesh Affairs. These are related to displacement, service pressure, gender and social inclusion, but should not be labelled as direct population-census questions.

## Recurring high-value themes to prepare

These are **priority themes inferred from the official syllabus and the archive-reported examples above**, not statistically proven frequency rankings:

1. Census: definition, methods, enumerated vs adjusted total.
2. Ethnic and cultural diversity of Bangladesh.
3. Demographic dividend and human-capital formation.
4. Education, health, skills and employment.
5. Urbanisation and internal migration.
6. Women's empowerment and labour-force participation.
7. National identity, equal citizenship and social cohesion.
8. Linguistic diversity, mother-tongue education and cultural heritage.
9. Rohingya displacement as a related migration/humanitarian theme.
10. Population ageing and future social protection.
11. Globalisation, ICT and intergenerational cultural change.
12. Inclusive public policy using Census and survey evidence.

## How the model answer bank was designed

The accompanying [25-question answer bank](./BCS-WRITTEN-QUESTION-BANK.md) includes:
- short definitions for 5/10 marks;
- causal and evaluative essays for 20 marks;
- Bangladesh-specific evidence and constitutional provisions;
- source lines from BBS, UNFPA, UNESCO and the Constitution;
- caveats against mixing census methods, treating projections as certainties, or inventing past-paper wording.

**Final exam rule:** Use a verified question's original wording and marks when available. Use model questions to cover the syllabus; never label a model prompt as an exact past question without evidence.
