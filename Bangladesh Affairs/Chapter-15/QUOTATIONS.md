## Gender Issues & Development | BCS Written

> **Use note:** Quotations are optional evidence, not substitutes for analysis. Verify wording and context from the cited source before using a quotation verbatim. The “exam-ready lines” below are original paraphrases and must not be placed in quotation marks as source quotations.

## A. Short quotations suitable for exam answers

1. **“All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights.”**\
   **Source:** United Nations, *Universal Declaration of Human Rights* (1948), Article 1.\
   **Use for:** constitutional equality, human rights, discrimination and dignity.\
   https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights

2. **“Women’s rights are human rights.”**\
   **Source:** United Nations, *Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action* (1995), widely associated with the Beijing Conference and its commitments.\
   **Use for:** women’s empowerment, violence prevention and equal citizenship.\
   https://www.unwomen.org/en/how-we-work/intergovernmental-support/world-conferences-on-women

3. **“Achieve gender equality and empower all women and girls.”**\
   **Source:** United Nations, Sustainable Development Goal 5 title.\
   **Use for:** SDG 5, national development planning and the 2030 Agenda.\
   https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal5

4. **Editorial paraphrase: The advancement of women and equality between women and men concern human rights and social justice.**\
   **Source guidance:** UN Beijing policy framework (1995). This wording is an editorial paraphrase; the earlier attribution to the opening commitments of the Declaration has not been established from the original text in this audit. Do not use it as a verbatim quotation.\
   **Use for:** equality as a rights-based obligation rather than charity.\
   https://www.unwomen.org/en/how-we-work/intergovernmental-support/world-conferences-on-women

5. **“The equal rights of men and women”**\
   **Source:** United Nations, *Charter of the United Nations* (1945), Preamble. This is a short excerpt, not a complete sentence.\
   **Use for:** international commitments and the historical foundations of equality.\
   https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text

6. **“Discrimination against women violates the principles of equality of rights and respect for human dignity.”**\
   **Source:** United Nations, *Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women* (CEDAW, 1979), Preamble.\
   **Use for:** gender discrimination, legal reform and women’s access to public life.\
   https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-elimination-all-forms-discrimination-against-women

7. **“Equal pay for work of equal value”**\
   **Source:** International Labour Organization, *Equal Remuneration Convention, 1951 (No. 100)*, Article 1. This is a treaty phrase, not a full sentence.\
   **Use for:** wage equality, decent work and women’s economic participation.\
   https://www.ilo.org/

8. **Thematic phrase: Decent work for all women and men**\
   **Source:** International Labour Organization, decent work framework; related to SDG 8. This is a thematic phrase, not a direct complete-sentence quotation.\
   **Use for:** employment quality, safety, labour rights and economic empowerment.\
   https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/decent-work

9. **“Leave no one behind.”**\
   **Source:** United Nations, *Transforming our world: the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development* (2015).\
   **Use for:** inclusive development, intersectionality and reaching marginalised women.\
   https://sdgs.un.org/2030agenda

10. **“End all forms of discrimination against all women and girls everywhere.”**\
    **Source:** United Nations, SDG target 5.1.\
    **Use for:** laws, institutions and anti-discrimination policies.\
    https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal5

11. **“Recognize and value unpaid care and domestic work”**\
    **Source:** United Nations, SDG target 5.4. This is an excerpt from the target.\
    **Use for:** unpaid care burden, time poverty, childcare and the care economy.\
    https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal5

12. **“Ensure women’s full and effective participation and equal opportunities for leadership”**\
    **Source:** United Nations, SDG target 5.5. This is an excerpt from the target.\
    **Use for:** women in politics, public administration and decision-making.\
    https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal5

13. **“Eliminate all forms of violence against all women and girls”**\
    **Source:** United Nations, SDG target 5.2. This is an excerpt from the target.\
    **Use for:** gender-based violence, safe workplaces and survivor-centred justice.\
    https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal5

14. **“Gender equality is not only a fundamental human right, but a necessary foundation for a peaceful, prosperous and sustainable world.”**\
    **Source:** United Nations, *Sustainable Development Goal 5: Gender Equality* explanatory text.\
    **Use for:** introduction or conclusion to an essay on gender equality and sustainable development.\
    https://www.un.org/sustainabledevelopment/gender-equality/

15. **Editorial synthesis: Women’s empowerment and gender equality are prerequisites for sustainable development.**\
    **Source:** Treat this as an exam-ready synthesis of UN sustainable-development principles, **not as a verified verbatim quotation**. Use without quotation marks or replace with a verified quotation above.\
    **Use for:** linking gender equality to national development.

## B. Constitution and Bangladesh-focused legal references

16. **Constitution of Bangladesh, Article 27:** “All citizens are equal before law and are entitled to equal protection of law.”\
    **Source:** *The Constitution of the People’s Republic of Bangladesh*, Article 27. Check the official text for exact punctuation/wording.\
    **Use for:** equality before law and equal protection.

17. **Constitution of Bangladesh, Article 28(2):** “Women shall have equal rights with men in all spheres of the State and of public life.”\
    **Source:** *The Constitution of the People’s Republic of Bangladesh*, Article 28(2).\
    **Use for:** constitutional basis of women’s participation and equality.\
    **Official portal:** https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/

18. **Constitution of Bangladesh, Article 29:** equal opportunity in public employment is a key constitutional principle.\
    **Source:** *The Constitution of the People’s Republic of Bangladesh*, Article 29. This is a paraphrase; consult the official text for a verbatim quotation.\
    **Use for:** public service and employment.

## C. How to use quotations in a written answer

- **Introduction:** use UDHR Article 1 or the UN SDG 5 framing.
- **Legal analysis:** use the Bangladesh Constitution (Articles 27–29) and CEDAW.
- **Economy and labour:** use ILO Convention No. 100 and SDG 5.4 on unpaid care.
- **Leadership:** use SDG 5.5 on participation and leadership.
- **Violence:** use SDG 5.2 and CEDAW.
- **Conclusion:** use “Leave no one behind” from the 2030 Agenda.

### Original exam-ready lines (paraphrases, not direct quotations)

- Gender equality is not a sectoral favour; it is a condition for inclusive human development.
- Empowerment requires not only access to resources but also the agency to make decisions and control outcomes.
- A law becomes transformative when enforcement, accessible services and social change reinforce one another.
- Measuring participation alone is insufficient; policymakers must also measure safety, pay, time use and decision-making power.
- Gender-responsive policy should be assessed by results, not merely by budget allocations or declarations.
